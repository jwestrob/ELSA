THE METHOD BEING CHECKED (unpublished; describe it to no one, just compare it with the literature)
Goal: find conserved gene neighbourhoods (operon-like loci, defence systems, secretion systems, biosynthetic clusters) across thousands of prokaryotic genomes, de novo, and type, map and search them.
D1. Genomes as text, loci as words. Each contig is a sequence of genes. Adjacent units are merged greedily, pair by pair, into nested "words", in the manner of byte-pair encoding or grammar induction, with the merge criterion being a description-length saving: the Poisson log-likelihood ratio n ln(n/E) - n + E of the number n of independent lineages showing the pair as neighbours against its expectation E under random gene order. The word cost (threshold) is calibrated on genomes with shuffled gene order.
D2. Gene identity without protein families. Instead of clustering proteins into families, each gene carries a list of similar genes in other genomes, taken from protein language model embeddings (mean-pooled, whitened), with a per-genome standardised (z-score) similarity threshold calibrated to a false-match rate. A word carries the set of spans in other genomes that supported it, so a word is in effect a progressive local alignment of a gene neighbourhood against all genomes at once, and its instances may differ in composition.
D3. Independence of evidence. An effective count N_eff = n / (1 + (n - 1) * s) for n genomes carrying a pair, where s is the mean fraction of gene adjacencies shared between the carrier genomes; analogous in form to effective sample sizes under equicorrelation and to sequence reweighting in covariation analysis.
D4. Phrases. Consecutive words are joined where a "cohesion" score is high: for a gene at a word boundary, take its homologs in other genomes and measure how similar (cosine of embeddings) their neighbours on the facing side are to each other. This resembles successor-variety or branching-entropy segmentation from linguistics, computed in embedding space.
D5. Locus vectors. One vector per locus, the mean of its genes' whitened protein-language-model embeddings; nearest-neighbour typing of loci (system type, subtype), clustering into a map of all loci, and fast similarity search over loci, including between loci that share no protein family.
D6. Validation by co-folding. Predicted neighbourhoods of unannotated genes are tested by predicting the structure of the encoded proteins as a complex (ESMFold-type or AlphaFold-Multimer-type), with partner-swap controls matched for length and hydrophobicity, adjacent-but-uncalled pairs, and curated system mates as controls, at class level (pass rates) and for individual blocks.
D7. A finding to position: similarity between protein-language-model embeddings beyond a sequence-clustering family is mostly homology that the clustering split; calibrated statistics (E-values) for embedding cosine similarity do not hold, while a fixed standardised threshold does.
