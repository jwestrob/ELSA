Encoding (aksha/initialize.py):
- KOs with score_type "full":   GA/TC/NC = thr      -1000.00  (sequence slot = threshold)
- KOs with score_type "domain": GA/TC/NC = -1000.00 thr       (domain slot = threshold)

Why -1000 (verified with pyhmmer 0.12 as patched by astra, and by reading the native code):
- -99999 is HMMER's p7_CUTOFF_UNSET. An optimized profile (.h3p) reads it as "cutoff missing", and the
  search raises MissingCutoffs (tested).
- -inf round-trips through text and pressed files, but the GPU path rejects it: adapter.py,
  _native.pyx and domain_rescore_cuda.cu all require finite GA. The GPU GA pruning uses only the
  sequence slot, as a reject when its upper bound < GA1. With GA1 = -1000 it never prunes. It reads
  GA2 only for its census statistics.
- -1000 survived text write and read, hmmpress, .h3m and .h3p reading, and a bit-cutoff search (tested).

Functions in aksha/initialize.py:
- ko_thresholds returns (KO, threshold, score_type); kofam_cutoffs builds the pair.
- add_threshold(path, thr, score_type) rewrites only when the values differ, through a .tmp file and a rename.
- stamp_kofam_cutoffs, repress_hmm_database, record_cutoff_encoding, restamp_kofam_database, local_ko_list.

Install and CLI behaviour:
- install_KOFAM stamps through stamp_kofam_cutoffs. It presses again when a rerun changed any cutoff
  and records the encoding.
- install_RP16 stamps all 16 markers from ko_list, including copies taken from an old-encoded KOFAM.
- New CLI flag: aksha initialize --restamp --hmms KOFAM,RP16. It reads the local ko_list in db_path
  (falling back to the database's parent directory), downloads nothing, presses again when a profile
  changed, and prints a reminder to regenerate the GPU manifest.
- Registry: each KOFAM or RP16 entry in ~/.config/Aksha/hmm_databases.json now gets
  "cutoff_encoding": "score_type". An entry without the field uses the old encoding.
- aksha/search.py adds warn_legacy_kofam_cutoffs. It prints and logs a WARNING when
  --cut_ga/--cut_tc/--cut_nc/--cascade (or --16rp) uses a KOFAM or RP16 entry that lacks the field.
  It was seen in the validation log of the production-database run.

Profiles with no cutoff:
- 1,318 KOFAM profiles have no threshold in ko_list. They stay unstamped, as before.
- With --cut_ga alone they report hits at Aksha's default E-value inclusion (0.01).
- With --cascade they are searched at E = 1e-15.

Other files changed: aksha/main.py (--restamp flag), docs/database-setup.md (KOfam thresholds

2. VALIDATION (16,000 SR-VP proteins, full KOFAM of 27,816 profiles)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Sample:
- 8,000 bacterial plus 8,000 archaeal proteins, drawn uniformly at random (seed 20261119) from the
  3,211,751 proteins in ws_srvp_bins/package; they come from 988 genomes.
- I chose 16,000 from a calibration: about 0.98 CPU-s per protein for a full KOFAM search, so 3
  searches were expected to take about 45 min on 24 CPUs.
- The job actually took 2 h 11 min (job 117732, node-64-768g-26). The first run spent 65 min loading
  the freshly pressed scratch copy from VAST (cold cache) plus 13 min in the cutoff-availability scan.
  The search phase itself took 11.6 min under the new encoding and 11.7 min under the production
  encoding.

Software and runs:
- Each run was "aksha search --installed_hmms KOFAM --threads 24", using the same branch build.
- (i)   production KOFAM (read-only, threshold in both slots), --cut_ga
- (ii)  re-stamped scratch copy, --cut_ga
- (iii) re-stamped copy with every bit threshold at -1000. KofamScan's rule was then computed from
        its output by compare_encodings.py: full score or best domain score, chosen by score_type,
        compared with the threshold.

Assignments, (sequence, KO) pairs among the 26,498 KOs with a threshold:
- (i)   production:               6,536
- (ii)  fix:                      6,588
- (iii) KofamScan rule:           6,587, plus 1 tie (below)
- Gained (ii) vs (i): 52, all score_type full (0 domain). Lost: 0.
- (i) equals "full >= thr AND best domain >= thr" computed from (iii), apart from the same tie.
  This confirms the old semantics.

Agreement of the fix with KofamScan's rule:
- They agree on all 6,588 pairs.
- The one listed difference is a tie: protein 1376737, K01992 (domain type, threshold 18.70).
  - Its best domain prints as 18.70 in the 2-decimal tables.
  - HMMER's float comparison counts it as >= 18.70, so both (i) and (ii) assign it.
  - My printed-score reference called it below the threshold. It is the only pair within 0.006 bits
    of its threshold.
- Row-level check: the rows of (ii) equal the (iii) rows that the rule implies (all domains for
  full-type pairs, domains >= threshold for domain-type pairs). There is 1 differing row, from the
  same tie.

Which proteins gain:
- 52 pairs on 44 proteins: 32 bacterial and 20 archaeal pairs.
- By shape: 46 multi-domain hits (2-17 domains, none reaching the threshold) and 6 single-domain
  hits whose best domain is below it.
- Full score minus threshold: median +21.2 bits (range +0.19 to +1,009.6).
- Best domain minus threshold: median -27 bits (range -440 to -0.06).
- These are mostly split alignments, which are far from a near-threshold rounding effect.
- 39 proteins gain their first KO, and 9 gains add a KO to an already-annotated protein. The best KO
  per protein, by full score, changes for 1 protein.
- Examples:
  - large transporters: 4 RND multidrug-efflux KOs on one 2,072-aa protein;
  - two-component sensors (FixL, K14986, on 1,547- and 1,559-aa proteins);
  - nitrate reductase alpha (4 domains);
  - leucyl-tRNA synthetase and topoisomerase I;
  - ribosomal protein L3 (K02906, an RP16 marker) on one archaeal protein;
  - one 1,358-aa Nitrospirota TPR-type protein that gains 5 KOs (tight adherence protein D, DnaJ C3,
    DnaJ C7, rhomboid GluP, APC3).

Rows:
- Total rows: 18,420 -> 18,707 (+287, +1.6%).
- All 288 new rows carry a domain score below the threshold: 181 belong to the 52 gained pairs and
  107 to 89 kept pairs.
- The lowest domain score among (ii) rows is -15.73.

Consumers that keep one row per (protein, KO):
- The best-domain row is identical in all 6,536 kept pairs.
- Taking the first row, or the highest full score with the first row breaking ties, picks a
  different row in 39 kept pairs.
- Sharur's kofam_scores keeps the best-domain row, so it is unaffected.
- Aksha's rp16 ranks by full score, so it picks the same protein.

The 1,318 threshold-less profiles: they produced 11,846 rows (657 KOs) under --cut_ga, identical in
(i) and (ii). They make up 63% of all rows in a KOFAM --cut_ga search.

Re-stamp cost (verified on the full scratch copy):
- aksha initialize --restamp --hmms KOFAM took 20 min 47 s on the login node, single-threaded.
- 26,498 profiles were rewritten (about 14 min), then 27,816 profiles were pressed again (394 s).
- Peak RSS was 3.6 GB.

5. POINTS AGAINST DEPLOYING, OR TO SETTLE FIRST
-----------------------------------------------
- KofamScan's full-score rule rewards repeat and split proteins. One TPR-type protein gains 5
  different KOs, and one 2,072-aa protein gains 4 paralogous RND KOs. The fix reproduces KofamScan
  exactly, so this is KofamScan's behaviour; consumers that treat every assigned KO as a functional
  call inherit it.
- Margin of the permissive value: the unthresholded search shows domain scores down to -698.41 bits
  (protein 1684679, K14308; 154 rows below -100). Under -1000, a domain scoring below -1000 inside an
  assigned full-type protein would lose its row; the assignment stays. A value such as -1e6 (finite,
  and different from -99999) removes that risk with a one-line change. It needs no new search, since
  every observed score is above -1000. The decision is yours before release.
- Consumers that keep the first row per (protein, KO) pick a different row in 39 kept pairs (0.6%).
  Best-domain consumers (Sharur) and full-score consumers (rp16) are unaffected.
- The production software drift in step a.
No result argues against the change itself: 0 assignments lost, and the fix matches KofamScan's rule
exactly.
