BOTTOM LINE
1. Lead 1 (Thy1, PF02511): SUPPORTED, rungs 2-5 all pass. The ~550-aa genes are consistent with a flavin-dependent
   thymidylate synthase (ThyX) built as a TANDEM DUPLICATION of the ThyX domain. The two halves keep COMPLEMENTARY halves
   of the active site: the N half keeps S88/R90 (26/26 genes), the C half keeps R78/R174 (26/26). In the experimental
   tetramer (PDB 1O26) these two residue sets come from two different chains. A homodimer of the long chain folds as
   the ThyX tetramer (TM 0.80-0.84) and puts FAD + dUMP into 2 complete composite pockets; the other 2 hold at most
   1 core residue. In 17/19 Pacearchaeales genomes it is the only dTMP-forming enzyme: 3.9 expected, Fisher p = 2.5e-9.
   Caveats for criterion B: this is a housekeeping enzyme, and only 5/32 genes are embedding-only (rung 7). The
   distinctive part is the half-active-site tandem architecture. The 1,138 two-domain annotated DPANN ThyX share it
   (59/60 sampled show the split), and it gives a testable prediction: 2 active sites per dimer.
2. Lead 2 (Thymidylat_synt, PF00303): KILLED as a lead (criterion B). It is a routine near-threshold miss. CDD's best hit
   is already in the thymidylate synthase family for 41/48 genes (E down to 2.4e-29). The Pfam scores sit just under the
   gathering threshold (12.0-24.7 bits against GA 24.3), and the alignment lists give the same label for 45/48.

  The negative-control dimer also takes ligands (27-29 contact residues), so the residue pattern carries the evidence;
