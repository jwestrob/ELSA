context_kernel workstream: REPORT (2026-11-12, DPANN, ESM++ small zca10 + gene order)
==========================================================================================

QUESTION
Do families built from one input (ESM++ small vectors) plus gene order, with similarity edges
weighted by neighbourhood agreement, K(a,a') = k(x_a,x_a') * sum_{d,d'} k(x_{a+d}, x_{a'+d'}),
serve the recurrence engine as well as MMseqs2 families, and do they remove the TXSScan loss of
context-free embedding families?

ANSWER IN FIVE LINES
1. The chosen context arm (dense w1 context gate at 1% FDR, weight sim x ctx; 880,124 tokens) matches the
   MMseqs2 base: summed DefenseFinder + TXSScan excess -0.004 [-0.022, +0.016] on held-out even genomes,
   TXSScan -0.013 [-0.024, -0.001] (context-free graph arms of round 1: -0.074 to -0.095). P1 and P2 met.
2. The work is done by the context GATE (edge selection) and needs true gene order. The context WEIGHTING
   adds nothing (same edges with weight sim: -0.014 summed, -0.023 TXSScan), and weighting all edges by
   sim x ctx hurts TXSScan (-0.114 to -0.211).
3. P3 fails: at edge level sim x ctx ranks same-type gold pairs far worse than sim alone
   (AUROC 0.487 vs 0.841 for dense_w1; -0.353 [-0.371, -0.335]). Context helps on one diagnostic only:
   TXSScan partners vs non-gold look-alikes (sim 0.608 -> sim x ctx 0.633 dense_w1, 0.751 matched_w5).
4. Exploratory, outside the predeclared pool: the context-free control V0 (same gene-level edges, weight
   sim, Leiden r19000, 297,708 tokens) beats the base on the summed criterion: tune +0.033, test +0.038
   [+0.017, +0.062], through DefenseFinder localization (+0.082) at lower F1 on every caller.
5. A strict context gate (matched w5) erases DefenseFinder systems (F1 0.060 vs 0.806): 58% of their
   genes have no supported edge, against 36% of all genes.

MECHANISM (token purity, results/purity.tsv; TXSScan member genes)
  mean token size / share of token-mates that are same-type TXSScan members:
  base 1,869 / 0.17; round-1 graph r3000 79 / 0.31; V0 r23000 33 / 0.31; V3 r26000 17 / 0.43 (18%
  singletons); V1b 1,408 / 0.20. TXSScan excess tracks keeping the large secretion families whole.
  This revises the round-1 reading: the context-free embedding families lose TXSScan by fragmenting
  large families (purity is higher than the base), and the true-order gate restores large components
  at low resolution. DefenseFinder localization rewards the opposite (small, precise tokens), which is
  why V0 and V3 gain DefenseFinder excess while losing TXSScan.

CAVEATS
  - Engine null and context-derived tokens (BRIEF trap 3): the engine shuffles tokens built from true
    order. Engine FDR is 0.0011-0.0080 for context arms, 0.0061-0.0089 for V0, 0.0044 for the base;
    significant pairs 109k-255k vs 160k. No visible inflation; the rigorous test (rebuild tokens on the
    shuffled order) was not run.
  - Token counts differ across the gate comparison (V1b 880k, V0 r30000 710k, permuted gate 677k).
  - Gold-pair labels for P3 treat homologs in different system types as negatives; for families this
    label is stricter than homology (Cas1 of two subtypes is one family).
  - Non-gold partners of TXSScan members include homologs in uncalled systems.
  - V0's summed gain is an excess (precision) gain at lower F1 on all three callers.
  - One exploratory engine job (permuted-order gate, sim x ctx, r50000, job 78664) sat single-threaded
    after the engine's rule stage for 10 min (its sim-weighted twin took 2 min) and was cancelled; the
    twin (r50000) and the sim x ctx r100000 arm answer the same control question.

SPEED
  Edge stage 529 s on 64 CPUs (vectors+zca10 92 s, expansion 4 s, union 64 s, context 63 s, null 77 s,
  FDR and gold diagnostics ~200 s). Leiden 1-3 min on supported graphs (19-41 M edges); 7-28 min on the

  Leiden on the full graph is the bottleneck; parallel runs share the graph by fork (14 runs, 96 GB).
