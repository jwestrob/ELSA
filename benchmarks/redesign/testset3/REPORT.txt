DECISION NEEDED — archaeal size
The predeclared rule gives 142 archaea, short of the 300 target. Two caps bind together:
  - Phylum cap 45 (15% of 300): Halobacteriota and Thermoproteota reach 45 each; every other archaeal phylum has fewer
    eligible genomes. Scan of the cap (metadata only): 45 -> 142, 60 -> 172, 70 -> 185, no phylum cap -> 186.
  - 4 genomes per family: with no phylum cap the archaeal pool still stops at 186 (159 complete + 27 fill), because
    complete and near-complete archaea sit in few families (54 families in the selection).
Reaching 300 needs more than 4 genomes per archaeal family, and the extra genomes would come mostly from Halobacteriota and
Thermoproteota (each already 32% of the 142).
Options:
  keep 142 (built now)      phylum and family caps as specified; archaea are 17% of genomes and 13% of proteins.
  lift the phylum cap       186 archaea (+44 genomes, about +100,000 proteins, about +10 min of A5000 time); the two
                            large phyla rise to about 35-40% of archaea.
  more per family           up to 300 with a per-family limit above 4 (not computed); concentrates the same two phyla.
Recommendation: keep 142. The set exists to test ribosomal-locus resolution; 142 archaea in 11 phyla with complete or
near-complete genomes already give 54 families, and the extra genomes would mainly add haloarchaea and Thermoproteota.
The package is built on this choice; a change means one selection rerun plus re-embedding of the added genomes.
