First test on untouched data: the lut set

Two configurations were frozen exactly as selected on DPANN and run once on genomes the method had never seen. The bond tree on embedding lists passed every criterion written down beforehand. The bond tree on protein families missed one.

  • The test set. 80 genomes with the complete lut cluster (all Methylobacterium), 578 with partial or split arrangements, 300 close relatives with no lut locus, and a thin background across Alphaproteobacteria. No ELSA method had been run on it.
  • Embedding-list tree: all four criteria. It holds each of the 80 complete clusters in one locus, and a nested locus isolates the transporter module in 75 of them.
  • Family-bond tree: three of four. Its excess is +0.149 [+0.138, +0.161] against a required lower bound of +0.15. It still beats the reference engine by +0.041 [+0.027, +0.057] and resolves 120 more ribosomal runs.
  • The order reversed. On DPANN the family-bond tree resolved more ribosomal runs. Here the embedding-list tree is ahead on every measure.
  • The tree matters. Plain pair-linking on the same bonds scores F1 0.58 and 0.47 and resolves 526 and 373 runs.
  • The published synton pipeline shows no signal and recovers no ribosomal run; its loci hold at most 12 genes.
  • Known weakness. Every locus holding a complete cluster runs past it: a median of 62% of its genes lie inside the cluster for the embedding-list tree. All 80 complete-cluster genomes fall in a single genome group out of 179, so the cluster's cross-lineage evidence is thin.
  • False-positive check. In the 300 relatives without a lut locus, isolated lut homologs are placed in loci no more often than background genes.

Reading the benchmark

Curated systems
Three purpose-built callers supply the truth set: DefenseFinder (defense systems), TXSScan (secretion systems) and CRISPRCasTyper (CRISPR-Cas loci). Each call with at least two member genes is one instance.
F1
For each instance, the best-overlapping predicted locus is scored at the gene level (harmonic mean of the share of the instance it covers and the share of the locus that belongs to the instance), then averaged over instances.
Excess score
F1 minus the F1 the same loci earn when placed at random positions. The overall score is the DefenseFinder excess plus the TXSScan excess. It rewards loci that fit systems and gives no credit for covering most of the genome.
Ribosomal locus
1,686 runs of at least 16 ribosomal-protein genes, a check that needs no curated caller. A run is recovered when one locus covers half of it and resolved when one locus covers 80% of it and at least 80% of that locus lies inside the run. 114 runs in the family Parvarchaeaceae are tracked separately, because they are the first to be lost when evidence is counted per lineage.
Seven bars
To match the reference: overall score within 0.01; each of the three F1 values within 0.02; at least 1,650 runs and 110 Parvarchaeaceae runs recovered; at least 1,400 runs resolved. To beat it, the overall-score difference must also have a 95% interval above zero.
Protocol
One output per method. Settings are selected on odd-numbered genomes and written down before any even-genome number is read. Differences are paired genome bootstraps (2,000 draws).

The algorithm

Two engines exist. The reference engine tests pairs of protein families and then applies reading rules; it stays the repository default and sets the bars. The candidate described here replaces the pair test and every reading rule with one objective, and it is the method all results on this page refer to as words.

  1. Give every gene an identity

    The method needs to recognize "the same gene" in another genome. Two sources are described here. A third, alignment hits used as lists, is the control reported under What embeddings add.

    Protein families

    MMseqs2 clusters (E ≤ 1e-3, 50% coverage), with families of fewer than three members joined to their best hit's family. Identity is a label, and two genes match when their labels are equal.

    Embedding neighbor lists (no families)

    Each protein's ESM++ small embedding is whitened so that cosine similarity is informative. For a gene and another genome, the best match enters the gene's list when it stands at least 10.6 standard deviations above that gene's similarities to the rest of that genome (about 1% false matches against non-homologs; cosine near 0.50). The median list holds 437 genes; 7% of genes have an empty list.

  2. Count how many independent lineages hold two neighbors together

    Every pair of neighboring units on a contig is a candidate, with up to two genes between them, in either order and on either strand. Its support n is the number of independent lineages in which the same two units are again neighbors. With family labels this is a sort over label pairs. With neighbor lists, a lineage supports the pair when an entry of one gene's list and an entry of the other's lie within two genes of each other on one contig.

  3. Compute how many lineages would hold them together by chance

    Under random gene order inside each genome, two units that are both common will sometimes land next to each other. The expectation E sums, over lineages, the probability that at least one genome of the lineage shows the pair within the window:

    E = Σlineages [ 1 − Πg in lineage ( 1 − min(1, 2w · cx(g) · cy(g) / mg) ) ]w = 3

    cx(g) is the number of copies of x in genome g (or list entries falling in g), and mg is the number of units in that genome.

  4. Score the merge with one statistic

    Λ = n ln(n / E) − n + Enats

    Λ is the Poisson log-likelihood ratio of seeing n lineages where E were expected. It equals the description length saved by recording those n occurrences as instances of one named word instead of n coincidences. A pair merges when n ≥ 2 and Λ ≥ τ, where τ is the cost of adding a word to the vocabulary.

  5. Commit the best merges and repeat

    Candidates are taken in decreasing Λ. A merge is committed when its genes are still unclaimed, so each gene belongs to one word. A committed pair, together with any one or two genes sitting between its halves, becomes a single unit that can merge again in the next round. Rounds continue until no pair passes (7 to 25 rounds observed). The result is a parse of every genome into nested words and background genes.

    The two identity sources differ in what a word is. With family labels, a word is an exact composition: the pair of its children's labels. With neighbor lists, a word carries the set of spans in other genomes that supported its creation, so each instance may have a different composition and different inserted genes. A word then grows by local alignment against all other genomes at once.

  6. Decide what counts as independent evidence

    This choice matters more than any other. Counting every genome separately lets shared ancestry pass for recurrence, and words chain into units hundreds of genes long. Two corrections have been tested.

    Genome groups

    Genomes are clustered by average linkage on the embedding similarity of their best-matching proteins and cut at 0.75, giving 642 groups (GTDB places these genomes in 922 genera). n counts groups. The cut was calibrated on the odd-numbered DPANN genomes, and a rule for setting it on a new dataset has yet to be found.

    Shared gene order (no groups, no cut)

    For two genomes, S is the share of one genome's adjacent pairs that are also adjacent in the other. The carriers of a pair then count as Neff = n / (1 + (n − 1) · s̄) independent observations, with s̄ their mean pairwise S. Λ is computed per genome and scaled by Neff / n; a merge needs Neff ≥ 2.

  7. Read loci from the parse

    The words, together with the sub-words nested inside them, are the loci. One further level handles long conserved neighborhoods, which differ in composition from lineage to lineage and therefore parse as several words in a row.

    Phrases. Consecutive words at most two genes apart are joined when the boundary between them is cohesive. Cohesion asks whether the homologs of a boundary gene keep the same kind of neighbor on the side facing the boundary: it is the mean pairwise cosine among those neighbors' embeddings, and a boundary takes the lower value of its two genes. Words are joined at cohesion ≥ 0.1, a value selected on odd-numbered genomes for six of nine vocabularies.

  8. Summarize each locus as one vector

    The mean of the whitened embeddings of a locus's genes is its vector. Nearest neighbors among these vectors give the locus a type, clustering them gives a map of all loci, and an index over them answers searches. Results are in Loci as vectors.

Cost

With family labels, one full run over 3.59 million genes takes 64 seconds on one core and 1.3 GB of memory; the shared-gene-order matrix adds under 20 seconds. With neighbor lists, building the lists takes 279 seconds and the merge rounds 529 seconds on 64 cores, excluding embedding and the similarity table. Work grows linearly with the number of genes, and no pair of genomes is ever aligned.

What it replaces

The reference engine takes pairs of family labels within three genes, counts the GTDB genera holding each pair, and tests that count against the same chance expectation with a Poisson tail. Each boundary between genes receives the strongest support of any significant pair spanning it. Loci are then read off by rule: runs of supported boundaries form a neighborhood, which is cut where family co-occurrence modules change and trimmed at its ends to give a nested core. Those rules carry fixed settings (support level, module resolution, trimming factor) and the count needs a taxonomy.

What the vocabulary looks like

With family labels: 25,018 word types and 479,370 top-level instances covering 42% of genes; median span 2 genes, 90th percentile 5, longest 36, nesting up to 7 levels. With neighbor lists: 516,845 top-level instances covering 57% of genes, 99th-percentile span 17. Shuffled genomes run through the full embedding procedure yield 1.6% as many word instances. The reference engine places 71% of genes in loci.

The two missing bars

CRISPR-Cas with family identity

Cas proteins are split across many small families, so the same Cas neighborhood looks like different label pairs in different lineages and never accumulates support. 45.5% of Cas member genes sit outside every family word, against 21.2% for DefenseFinder systems and 19.9% for TXSScan systems.

The embeddings see through the split: a Cas gene's 1,000 nearest neighbors hold about 200 Cas genes from other families, against 2.7 for matched control genes. Identity has to be broad before the first merge. Grouping finished words into classes afterwards changes nothing (−0.003 [−0.007, +0.001]).

Ribosomal loci with embedding identity

A word built from neighbor lists keeps only the instances in other genomes that match along its whole length, and that set shrinks with every merge: a median of 224 aligned spans at two genes, 9 at ten genes and 2 at twenty. Two failure modes follow, depending on the lists.

Depth-limited lists fragment long neighborhoods: 322 of 598 unresolved ribosomal runs are under-covered. The calibrated one-match-per-genome lists do the opposite. Words span the whole ribosomal locus and continue into its conserved flanks, and 420 of 503 unresolved runs fall below 80% precision.

Joining words where the gene pair at the junction passes the merge test recovers all 1,686 runs and resolves 1,138, fewer than before. Recurrence is the only boundary signal the objective has, and conserved flanks recur too. The missing ingredient is a boundary signal: a cut where the aligned instances diverge in what flanks them, which is what cohesion measures.

The two independence measures pull in opposite directions on these bars. Genome groups favor the overall score and long neighborhoods; shared gene order favors CRISPR-Cas and rare systems. With family words and phrases, shared gene order reaches CRISPR-Cas F1 0.430 and 1,326 resolved runs, short of both bars at once.

Second dataset: Spacedust's benchmark

With its DPANN settings unchanged, the word method runs and finds signal on 1,317 mostly complete bacterial genomes. It does not beat Spacedust there, and simple linking over its own pair statistic does.

  • The procedure transfers. The 1% shuffle rule lands on the same thresholds as on DPANN (8 and 10 nats), and both word outputs beat random placement.
  • The parse is too short for these loci. Words span a median of 3 genes; the regions span a median of 10. Half of the gold genes lie outside every word, and the best single word covers a median 33% of a region. Excess is +0.08 to +0.15 on regions of up to 10 genes and near zero above that.
  • Linking alone does better. Connected runs of significant first-round pairs, with up to two genes between them, beat the word parse by +0.072 to +0.093 and beat Spacedust on excess by +0.077 to +0.088, at F1 0.58 against 0.68.
  • The closest published method does poorly outside its home setting. The synton pipeline of Hervé, Cury and Bernheim shows no signal here with its own 50%-identity families, which leave 54% of genes as singletons, and ties the word output when given our families. On DPANN it reports almost nothing, because its statistic assumes long replicons.
  • Neither independence correction is invariant to duplicated genomes. Copying 26 genomes nine times inflates their word types 8-fold under raw counting. Genome groups shrink them to 0.75 of the original and shared-gene-order counts to 0.83, with the vocabulary shifting. The chance expectation needs the same redundancy treatment as the count.

Redesign for long loci: the bond tree (development results)

A word has to recur with the same composition, which makes short loci precise and stalls long ones. The redesign drops word identity. Genes are linked by first-round significant bonds; each bonded run is split recursively at its weakest boundary, low-cohesion boundaries first, into a tree of nested intervals; nodes that pass the bond test as a whole are the loci.

DPANN: 1,598 of 1,686 ribosomal runs resolved, +86 [+60, +115] over the reference; overall score 0.584 against 0.427 for the reference and 0.624 for words; CRISPR-Cas F1 0.419, still below the bar. Spacedust's benchmark: +0.126, level with pair-linking, above Spacedust, below the reference engine.

Cost of the two list sources. Embedding lists for 4.2 M sequences took 57 minutes: two passes of about 20 minutes on one A5000 and 18 minutes on 64 CPUs. Alignment lists were projected at 12 to 18 hours on 64 CPUs. On DPANN the two score the same apart from CRISPR-Cas, so embeddings supply list identity at a small fraction of the cost.

Two parts of the first design failed. Cutting at low cohesion deletes real loci, because defense systems and biosynthetic regions have low cohesion inside; cohesion works as the order of splitting. A whole-locus recurrence test had nothing to calibrate against, since shuffled genomes produce almost no nodes.

What the ribosomal benchmark can show. An oracle that reports each whole conserved neighborhood resolves 1,507 runs under the standing definition, because 179 runs make up less than 80% of their own neighborhood. The reference's 174 failures are 157 loci that extend past the run. Passing 1,507 needs a nested output that also holds the run-sized piece.

Independent audit of the failures. A separate agent classified 120 sampled failures against a rubric written beforehand. It attributes about three quarters to the benchmark definition and one quarter to the method: reference 80% / 20%, family words with phrases 82% / 15%, embedding words 58% / 40%. One rubric rule was corrected after the fact, so these shares are post hoc. The commonest artifacts are loci that take in flanking families found beside ribosomal runs in many lineages (SecY in 274 groups), nearby ribosomal genes outside the run, and a scoring rule that picks the largest overlapping locus even when a nested locus fits the run. Under a definition that allows such flank and splits runs at junctions unique to one lineage, the three outputs resolve 1,571, 1,622 and 1,490, against 1,512, 1,420 and 1,172 under the standing one.

The bond tree under the same audit. It leads under the standing definition (1,598) and under every alternative (1,620 when any fitting locus counts; 1,671 under the relaxed definition). Its 56 under-covered runs are cut at junctions that recur in no other lineage above chance, mostly rearranged gene orders. The relaxed definition should be read with care: annotation alone supports only 9–13% of the flank it forgives, so the definition shares above are upper bounds. With independent support required, the bond tree still has the fewest errors: 44, against 70 for the reference, 159 for family words and 288 for embedding words.

Loci as vectors

Averaging a locus's gene embeddings into one vector is enough to type it, map it and search for it.

  • Map. 500,842 loci fall into 1,206 clusters whose agreement with curated types equals that of the reference engine's module labels (adjusted mutual information 0.510 against 0.518). 306 dense clusters contain no curated system; 57 of them are mostly unannotated and 30 of those span five or more genome groups.
  • Division of labour. Words give boundaries. Typing by word identity is pure and fragmented (homogeneity 0.993, completeness 0.420), and searching by parsing a query into words finds a tenth of the targets. Vectors do both jobs.
  • Limits. Vector similarity has no calibrated significance yet, and the index ranks conserved loci that no caller has annotated above some curated targets.

What embeddings add over alignment

Embedding similarity beyond the family mostly recovers homology that sequence clustering had split. Its distinctive contribution is a match for a protein with no alignable relative, anchored by a conserved neighbor.

  • The split is mechanical. 59% of the ten nearest non-family neighbors align directly at E ≤ 1e-3, and 87% of those alignments cover at least half of both proteins: full-length homologs that set-cover clustering filed under different representatives.
  • Similarity between unrelated proteins of the same kind is a small component, 2.0 to 2.4 times the baseline rate among pairs with no homology evidence.
  • The co-folding signal needs homology on at least one side. Pairs whose support is free of homology on both sides pass 1 time in 149. Where one chain has no alignable homolog in the dataset, pairs pass 8 times in 108 against 0 in 168 (analyzed after the fact).
  • Cosine similarity carries no usable E-value (extreme-value estimates run 8 to 42 times over nominal). A fixed standardized cut does: z ≥ 10.6, 13.7 and 16.0 give false-match rates of 10⁻², 10⁻³ and 10⁻⁴, stable across genome halves.
  • Control: alignment hits as the source of the lists. MMseqs2 all-against-all hits (E ≤ 1e-3), run through the identical word procedure, score +0.041 [+0.021, +0.062] over the reference. That is level with the calibrated embedding lists (difference −0.007 [−0.026, +0.014]) and +0.031 [+0.013, +0.050] above depth-limited embedding lists. For discovering loci, embeddings are one of two equally good sources of identity; the gain comes from dropping the hard family partition. Their distinct uses here are locus vectors and the anchored matches above.

Tested and set aside

  • Patching the embedding-only pair engine to parity. Four rounds reached the bars only through a second evidence tier added after the fact. This motivated the new method.
  • Context vectors as the measure of independence. The size of a conserved unit confounds it: pairs inside the ribosomal locus are discounted 30- to 90-fold.
  • A clustered embedding alphabet in place of families. −0.101 [−0.125, −0.076] against family words. The objective picks the coarsest clusters, which blurs locus edges.
  • Classes of words. Description length rises with every loosening and typing stays where it was (0.473 against 0.464).
  • Finer units of evidence than genome groups. Species-scale groups, per-genome weights and random partitions all fail, because a shuffle null is blind to inherited gene order.
  • Covariation between partner embeddings as a predictor of co-folding. AUROC 0.573.
  • Recognizing a kind of system with all of its families withheld. AUROC falls from 0.788 to 0.595.
  • E-values and hub corrections for cosine similarity. Both rank worse than the raw score.

Position against prior work

Two independent literature searches with live web access, citing only sources they opened, rate the method as known ingredients in a new combination.

Closest published method

Hervé, Cury and Bernheim, microLife, September 2026, from the group behind DefenseFinder. Poisson enrichment of family adjacency, clusters of similar genomic contexts as the counting unit, loci as connected runs of significant adjacencies, 32,791 RefSeq genomes. Their pipeline is being run on our data.

Already published

Effective genome counts (CSBFinder-S 2020; Junier and Rivoire 2016). The shuffle null and its blindness to ancestry (Durand and Sankoff 2003; Gecko 3). Mean-pooled operon vectors (SAFPred 2024). Co-folding of operon partners at scale (Qi et al. 2026).

No close precedent found

Greedy merging of gene order into a nested parse scored by lineage-adjusted enrichment. The effective count built from shared gene adjacency. Boundary cohesion. The finding that beyond-family embedding similarity is mostly split homology, with a standardized threshold where E-values fail.

Comparators reviewers will expect

Spacedust (Zhang, Mirdita and Söding 2025), Gecko 3, CSBFinder-S, the Hervé procedure, Gaia and gLM2 for neighborhood retrieval, SAFPred and BiG-SLiCE vectors for typing.
